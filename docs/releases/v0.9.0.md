# KISEKI v0.9.0

Devices and years. v0.9 is about what happens to a personal library
over time: the promises it makes are now checked by machine, a night
away is one journey instead of three days of them, anything the owner
wants gone is gone everywhere it spoke, and what a decade should look
like is a rule they can read and leave switched off.

## Highlights

- **The promises are checked by machine** (ADR-0059). Six privacy
  claims moved from the README into a test suite. `kiseki demo` --
  which ingests, builds, reads and derives -- runs with the socket
  constructor replaced by one that raises, so a future dependency that
  dials out fails before it reaches anyone. The screen reading is
  checked for a text field it must not have, the export's forbidden
  list runs against a profile containing an identifier, a place and an
  exact time, the blur has its number in the test, and no database or
  image may sit under `packages/` or `tests/`.
- **A night away is one journey** (ADR-0060). Outings split on
  silence, and sleep is silence: three days in Seoul arrived as three
  outings and everything downstream read them as three visits to a
  place the owner kept returning to. A trip is a run of outings at
  least fifty kilometres from every regular place, no more than
  thirty-six hours apart, spanning a night. Outings are untouched -- a
  trip is derived on top of them. `kiseki trips`.
- **A place knows which of its visits were on a trip.** `suggest` no
  longer offers to go back somewhere every visit was a holiday. This
  catches what a span cannot: an airport on the way to every trip has
  eight visits over two years and a cadence as tidy as any habit.
- **Forgetting reaches everything that spoke** (ADR-0061). Removing a
  photograph used to leave its caption, its subjects, its screen
  reading, its indexed text and its embedding exactly where they were,
  so `ask` could quote a photograph the owner had deleted. One module
  names the whole path; `kiseki forget` counts it, shows it, and only
  `--apply` removes it. Journeys are absent deliberately -- they are
  derived, and a rebuild produces a history without them.
- **Retention is a rule, not a machine** (ADR-0062). Photographs older
  than a span, refusals older than a span, kept readings thinned to
  the recent few plus one a month -- which keeps the shape of a decade
  rather than a window of it. Every rule is off unless the owner sets
  it, nothing runs on a timer, and photographs leave through the same
  path a deliberate deletion takes.

## What the data said

- The first real use of `forget` arrived the day it was written: six
  synthetic photographs from an old experiment were sitting in a real
  library, inventing a place that nobody had been to. The dry run
  showed exactly what would go; the library went from 4,956
  photographs to 4,950.
- Two years resolved into eight trips: Makubetsu, Seoul, Hirara,
  Guerande for eight nights, Hikone, Nikko, Nagasaki, Kushimoto.
- Forty-six kept readings would thin to nine under a modest policy,
  and the offer to do so is left where it belongs -- with the owner.

## Upgrading

- No schema change: v0.8 databases work as they are (version 5).
- New commands: `trips`, `forget`, `retention`.
- `kiseki forget` and `kiseki retention` both count and show before
  they do anything. Neither runs unless asked.

## Deferred, on purpose

Merging several devices is written into proposals/0007 and stays
there: this library has been read on one device, and shipping a
deduplication nobody can test against real data would be a guess
wearing a feature's clothes. It arrives when there is a second device
to try it on, or alongside v0.10's record boundary, whichever comes
first.
